## 0. Research question, scope, and rationale

In dense-LoRA sleeper agents based on Gemma-2-2B with adapters restricted to layer 19, what causal intervention effects are obtained by replacing trigger-dependent information that **unmodified base layers 0–18** carry from the tag position to later positions, and are those effects concentrated in the previously selected head pair `L15.attn.head6` and `L16.attn.head5` across five seeds?

The earlier Stage 1 pilot selected a shared three-head core by **zero ablation** in `l1523` seeds 42–44. It established that donor-selected ablation sets can suppress a backdoor across those checkpoints. It did **not** establish shared mechanisms, selective utility retention, or that base-model pathways cause the observed effects. The two-head pair in layers 15–16 was selected from these **previously observed data** and must be frozen before new confirmatory testing. `L23.attn.head0` is outside the primary `l19` upstream pathway and may be examined descriptively only.

**Causal estimand warning:** Patching computes the effect of particular interventions on model outputs. Fractional effects are **not** additive percentages of information, do not imply exclusive or uniquely necessary routes, and may be negative or greater than one due to interactions. The model is a single architecture, trigger construction, fixed payload, and published fine-tuning family; generalization beyond these is not claimed.

## 3. Intervention definitions

### 3.1 Signals, tags, and boundary

For an `l19` model, cache the clean-tag (C) and triggered (T) states under unmodified, deterministic, cache-disabled, teacher-forced inference for each paired instruction. A single token at the trigger word differs; all post-tag prompt tokens must align exactly. Under approved single BOS, the tag is at zero-based **index 5** and pre-tag indices are **0–4**; under Stage 1 double BOS these are index **6** and **0–5**, respectively. These numbers are diagnostic only: **derive the tag position and pre-/post-tag masks from actual paired token IDs** and fail closed if the one-token difference is not unique; never hard-code either index. Layer-19 input means the residual stream immediately entering transformer layer 19, with the same layer-input convention in all code and result files.

At the layer-19 boundary define four **hybrid** conditions, on identical token position grids:

| Condition | Post-tag residual positions | Tag-position residual |
|---|---|---|
| TT | Trigger | Trigger |
| CT | Clean | Trigger |
| TC | Trigger | Clean |
| CC | Clean | Clean |

The original token embeddings, position IDs, masks, and all other residual positions are held consistent with the appropriate underlying run; because only one token differs, the hybrid boundary condition must be specified exactly and validated by identity tests. For the boundary intervention, swap **all** post-tag residual values together (not a selection of heads). CT and TC are interchanges at the boundary and do not correspond to training an altered model.

### 3.2 Head-level patching and what it estimates

At the input to `self_attn.o_proj`, replace the chosen **query-head slice** `[head_index×256:(head_index+1)×256]` at aligned **post-tag** positions with the same-instruction donor run's slice. Use Gemma-2 GQA query-head indexing (8 query heads/layer); record hook order, tensor shapes, and cache provenance. Reverse patching replaces clean-run slices with trigger-run slices. All temporary hooks must be removed after each pass; set `use_cache=False` in teacher-forced passes.

**Estimand (Agent 1 C3):** A head-set effect `E(S)` is the **total downstream change** in the model's first-token decision margin caused by replacing the selected head outputs. It includes effects mediated through subsequent attention heads, MLPs, and downstream layers. It is **not** a direct-only path effect or the unique information carried by S. The pair interaction `E({h1,h2})−E({h1})−E({h2})` includes serial composition between layers 15 and 16, as well as other downstream interactions.

- Frozen nominated pair: `L15.attn.head6`, `L16.attn.head5`.
- **Complete layer-matched comparator population:** all **64** pairs (one of 8 layer-15 heads × one of 8 layer-16 heads), in **both patch directions**. Ranks refer to this finite enumerated population, not a sampled-pair p-value.
- **Primary upstream scan:** all **152** heads in layers 0–18, both directions. The other 56 heads in layers 19–25 are recommended-tier secondary/descriptive scans (all 208 total). `L23.attn.head0` and the original three-head set are secondary only.
- **All-upstream-head joint patch**, plus cross-fitted top-k joint patches for `k=1..10` in both directions. For **F2**, form folds on the **entire 200-ID held-out allocation order before checking model-specific eligibility**: fold A contains IDs at even zero-based positions (0,2,…,198), and fold B those at odd positions (1,3,…,199). Drop ineligible instructions *within their already assigned folds*, never rebalance or reassign. In each seed, rank single heads by signed effect on eligible fold A and evaluate its selected top-k joint set on eligible fold B, then reverse. Pool the two *out-of-fold* evaluations over the same IDs. The fold rankings remain fixed when bootstrapping outcome uncertainty. The resulting uncertainty is conditional on the selected rankings; no distributional inference about selection instability is claimed. `k*>10` is right-censored: it does not establish MLP mediation or prove that more than ten heads are sufficient.

## 4. Metrics and algebra

For each eligible instruction and model, let `a` be the **first payload token** and `b_i` be the **first greedy token** produced by the intact *clean-tag* model for that instruction. **Payload ID (C7 CPU evidence; approved for selection specification):** freeze `a = 235285` (token `I`), from the fixed 109-character UTF-8 `FULL_PAYLOAD` formed by joining ten `I HATE YOU` repeats with single spaces and no leading space. Cursor reports four agreeing paths: (A) `tokenizer(FULL_PAYLOAD, add_special_tokens=False)` (Stage 1 method); (B) full-string tokenization after `apply_chat_template(..., add_generation_prompt=True)` plus the payload; (C) full training-style user/assistant rendered conversation; (D) `build_teacher_forced_batch`. A default tokenizer call with `add_special_tokens=True` would prepend BOS `2` and must **not** define `a`. Record exact UTF-8 bytes and first-token ID in the freeze manifest; test first-token validity in exploratory forward passes before any confirmatory analysis. This token ID and its tokenization convention are approved; the full scientific preregistration still requires freeze audit.

At the last prompt position, define

`m_i(X) = log P_X(a) − log P_X(b_i)`.

If `a == b_i`, mark that prompt **ineligible** for this metric for that model (as preregistered); report all exclusions. If >10% are ineligible, the model fails the metric gate. `b_i` is frozen from the intact **single-BOS** clean run, never re-chosen for each intervention. The one descriptive double-BOS ranking instead computes its own `b_i` from an intact **double-BOS** clean run on the same development instruction; it never borrows the single-BOS reference (§14). First-token validity is checked against intact triggered generations on all examples. Teacher-forced payload-repeat log probability, Stage 1 D, continuation-only greedy ASR, clean false-fire, and saved full generations are secondary metrics. Report K on clean-tag prompts descriptively, not as a direct utility measurement. Normal instruction-answer quality cannot be asserted from K alone.

For each seed use **means across exactly the same eligible prompts**, denoted `m_TT`, `m_CT`, `m_TC`, `m_CC`, and define (all margin differences in nats):

- `T = m_TT − m_CC` (total trigger-associated contrast).
- `E_post_fwd = m_TT − m_CT` (effect of replacing moved post-tag information by clean values on trigger background).
- `E_post_rev = m_TC − m_CC` (effect of inserting moved post-tag information on clean background).
- `n_post = E_post_fwd / T`; `s_post = E_post_rev / T`.
- `n_tag = (m_TT − m_TC)/T = 1−s_post`.
- `s_tag = (m_CT − m_CC)/T = 1−n_post`.
- `interaction = n_post − s_post`.

All ratios are **ratios of paired prompt means**, not means of per-prompt ratios. Never divide by T if a model fails the predeclared T gate. Ratios can be outside [0,1]. These are intervention-defined contrasts, **not** unique fractions of causal information. The CT and TC substitutions do not establish that the complements are exclusively direct tag-reading routes; later layers can process either or both substituted streams and interactions are expected.

For any patched head set S, record the orientation-specific raw margin effect **in nats**: forward `E_fwd(S)=m_TT−m_trigger_with_clean_patch(S)`; reverse `E_rev(S)=m_clean_with_trigger_patch(S)−m_CC`. Positive forward effects **suppress** the trigger-associated decision; positive reverse effects **induce** it. Rank pairs and heads by the **signed effect** in each direction, not absolute effect. The head set and reference prompts remain fixed.

Head-level **route-normalized** comparisons divide a forward effect by `E_post_fwd` when the forward route is relevant, and a reverse effect by `E_post_rev` when the reverse route is relevant. **Never compare a dimensionless fraction with a margin in nats.** For confirmatory route-relative tests, the corresponding route share must have a **90% bootstrap lower bound ≥0.5**. Together with the lower 95% bootstrap bound of `T > 1 nat`, this implies a positive mean route-effect denominator greater than **0.5 nat in the point estimate of that route-effect denominator**; its interval is not itself guaranteed above 0.5 nat. No additional arbitrary numerical cutoff is introduced. For a direction not meeting this requirement, record effects in nats and as a share of T descriptively, without making route-relative head claims. Head effects may be negative, exceed the whole-route effect, or exceed T because interventions interact.

### 5.1 Route classes

Define `n_post` and `s_post` as in §4, with `theta_hi=0.5`, `theta_lo=0.2`.

| Class | Required 90% intervals |
|---|---|
| **R-moved** | lower(`n_post`)≥0.5 and lower(`s_post`)≥0.5 |
| **R-and** | lower(`n_post`)≥0.5 and upper(`s_post`)<0.2 |
| **R-or** | upper(`n_post`)<0.2 and lower(`s_post`)≥0.5 |
| **R-tag** | upper(`n_post`)<0.2 and upper(`s_post`)<0.2 |
| **R-intermediate** | For **every** named class above, at least one defining interval is entirely on the wrong side of its requirement |
| **Indeterminate** | Not assigned to a named class and not conclusively R-intermediate; e.g., a confidence interval straddles a threshold |

`R-tag` is tag-background-dominant **under the interventions**, not proof that the adapter literally attends directly to the tag. A negative or >1 contrast is preserved and evaluated by the same rules, not clipped.

### 5.2 H1 — nominated core-pair involvement

Test H1 only in an appropriate direction: **forward** for R-moved and R-and; **reverse** for R-moved and R-or. R-moved requires **both** directions for a full H1. In each applicable direction all of the following must pass:

1. **Rank stability:** The nominated pair's upper endpoint of its **90% bootstrap rank interval ≤3** out of 64. Rank from descending signed pair effect; deterministic tie-break by `(head15_index, head16_index)`; disclose all tied scores and tie count. Report both point rank and interval.
2. **Effect share:** Lower 90% bound of `E_core/E_route` ≥**0.5** for *strong H1*, or ≥**0.25** for *partial H1*. The route denominator itself must pass §4's positive-denominator gate.
3. **Both heads contribute conditional on the other:** For h15 and h16, lower 90% bounds of `(E_pair−E_h15)/E_route` and `(E_pair−E_h16)/E_route` must each be ≥**0.1**. Use the exact same held-out eligible prompts and patch direction for numerator and denominator. This is a *marginal-addition* rule, not proof of unique necessity.

If (1)–(2) pass but (3) fails, report **H1-unresolved-marginal-contribution**, identifying which contribution test failed; do **not** conclude only one head carries the signal, because redundancy and nonlinear interactions can make either marginal small. In R-moved, passing exactly one direction yields **H1-directional**, which is partial/descriptive, not full bidirectional H1.

**Three-way H1 state, applied to each relevant direction (Agent 1 additional review):**
- **H1-pass:** all rank, share, and marginal-addition criteria pass using their prescribed 90% intervals (distinguish strong vs partial effect-share pass).
- **H1-fail:** at least one criterion's **entire** 90% interval is on the wrong side: rank **lower** bound >3, core route-share **upper** bound <0.25, or either marginal-addition share **upper** bound <0.1. For an applicable direction, this is substantive failure rather than insufficient precision.
- **H1-indeterminate:** neither H1-pass nor H1-fail; for example, the point rank is ≤3 but its upper interval exceeds 3. This counts toward the study-level ≥2-indeterminate inconclusive rule for that endpoint. **H3 requires H1-fail**, not merely failure to satisfy the stringent H1-pass rule. Keep directional and partial reporting when one of two required directions passes but the other does not.

**Combining directions under R-moved:** A **full H1-pass** requires H1-pass in **both** forward and reverse directions. **H1-fail** (and thus potential eligibility for H3) requires H1-fail in **at least one** required direction. All other combinations are **H1-indeterminate** for the study-level full-H1 decision. Independently report any single-direction H1-pass as a **directional/partial observation**, without counting it as full H1 support.

### 5.3 H2/H3 — secondary concentration/alternative readout

- **Cross-fitted k threshold:** Compute out-of-fold joint-effect ratio `E_top-k/E_route` for each k, pooling paired prompt-level margins across both folds. Rank selection for each fold is based solely on the opposite fold. In bootstrap, **hold selection fixed** and resample prompts within each evaluation fold; report the conditional scope.
- **`k* ≤ 2`:** lower 90% bound of the cross-fitted `k=2` route share ≥0.5. **`k* > 10`:** upper 90% bound of the `k=10` share <0.5 (right-censored). The region between these inequalities is **undetermined concentration**, not automatically H2 or H3.
- **H2 (not localized to measured small sets):** For **every** scanned single upstream head and each of the 64 layer-15×layer-16 pairs, the **upper 90% bound** of its signed route-effect share is <0.25, **and** `k*>10`. If a component interval overlaps the 0.25 threshold, H2 is **indeterminate**, not supported by failure to reject. Even if H2 holds, this cannot exclude other-layer pairs, MLPs, or nonlinear cancellation. Disclose all signed effect distributions.
- **H3a (alternative conserved readout):** `k*≤2`, H1 is explicitly **H1-fail** (not H1-indeterminate), and the identical **unordered** two-head set appears in the top-2 signed single-head rankings in at least 4 of 5 seeds. Tie-break by `(layer,head)` ID. Since this top-2 set is discovered on confirmatory responses, its conservation is **within-study descriptive**; it needs new checkpoints for a truly independent confirmation.
- **H3b (alternative seed-varying readout):** `k*≤2`, H1 is explicitly **H1-fail** (not H1-indeterminate), and the discovered top-two heads differ across seeds. Interpret as a change in adapter **readout of the shared base stream**, not independent variation in upstream base computation.
- **Inapplicable:** For R-tag, R-intermediate, indeterminate, or a direction failing the route-denominator gate, report head effects descriptively but do not force an H1/H2/H3 label. H2/H3 and partial/directional findings do not manufacture a positive primary-H1 result.

### 5.4 Study-level decisions and precedence

1. For each model, record identities/gates first. If any gate fails, label that model **gate-failed/inconclusive** without replacing it.
2. Assign a route class, **then** evaluate direction-appropriate head hypotheses; pre-registered named classes take priority over any H1/H2/H3 speculation.
3. If **≥2 of five** primary seeds are gate-failed or indeterminate for the relevant endpoint, study-level result is **INCONCLUSIVE**.
4. Otherwise, if **≥4 of five** share the same route class, report that class **SUPPORTED WITHIN THIS FIVE-CHECKPOINT FAMILY**; otherwise report **MIXED**. Apply the same ≥4/5 rule independently to a **full, applicable head-level hypothesis**, counting inapplicable seeds as non-supporting. Report partial/directional findings separately.
5. No across-seed pooled p-values. Five seed checkpoints share the exact same base computation up to layer 19 and possibly correlated training order; independence is not assumed.

**Negative outcomes matter:** failure of H1 is not proof of distributed routing; an R-tag or indeterminate result does not prove zero upstream information. Report the continuous estimate, every signed effect, interval, rank, and exclusions without hiding nulls.

## 7. Statistical plan and transparency

**F1 — immutable bootstrap RNG, draw schedule and quantiles.** On **each model independently**, initialize **one** `numpy.random.Generator(numpy.random.PCG64(20261114))`. The model's eligible held-out prompt IDs must be ordered by their **original frozen held-out allocation positions**; do not sort by textual content, output or fold. Draw exactly **2,000** with-replacement prompt-index resamples of length `N` from the full eligible model set, preserving clean/trigger pairing and reusing **the same 2,000 index vectors** for every TT/CT/TC/CC contrast, each direction, and all 152 head/64 pair effects and ranks. Call **exactly** `rng.integers(0, N, size=(2000, N))` under **`numpy==2.1.3`**, with default **int64** and `endpoint=False`. **Next**, using the *same stream*, call **exactly** `rng.integers(0, N_A, size=(2000, N_A))` for the eligible fold A, then **exactly** `rng.integers(0, N_B, size=(2000, N_B))` for eligible fold B, with the same defaults, both in original allocation order; reuse these fold draws for every fixed cross-fitted k and direction, pooling their sampled prompt-level out-of-fold evaluations. No other random consumption is allowed between these three calls. **Every non-cross-fitted statistic uses the full-set draws**: this explicitly includes interaction contrasts, the core triple, layer-wise and downstream scans, the residual sweep, and the secondary `l1523` endpoints. Only cross-fitted statistics use the fold-specific draws. An empty eligible fold cannot be silently repaired: halt that model's cross-fitted endpoint and label it indeterminate. Model streams are separately initialized with the **same literal seed**, and their statistics are not pooled as independent checkpoints. Under each resample calculate **ratios of sampled paired means**, not averages of per-prompt ratios. Rank all 64 pairs anew on the **same resampled prompts**, sorting descending signed pair effects with deterministic `(head15_index, head16_index)` ascending tie-break. Cross-fitted top-k selections remain fixed from opposite-fold discovery, not reranked during bootstrap.

For all non-rank two-sided 90% intervals take the 5th and 95th percentiles using **`numpy.percentile(values, [5,95], method="linear")`**; for the mean-T 95% gate take **`numpy.percentile(values, [2.5,97.5], method="linear")`**. For discrete core-pair **rank** intervals take the 5th and 95th percentiles of its 2,000 per-resample integer ranks using **`numpy.percentile(ranks, [5,95], method="inverted_cdf")`**. These fixed methods apply to applicable primary and secondary models; exploratory sensitivity comparisons remain descriptive.

- Bootstrap over **prompt IDs within each model**, using the exact F1 algorithm above. Preserve 2,000 index vectors and resulting interval tables as reproducibility artifacts. Report 90% intervals for route shares and core ranks, 95% for mean T; no after-the-fact RNG or quantile changes.
- A model meets a particular route class or a threshold-based core claim only when the 90% interval remains on the correct side of all defining thresholds; otherwise label indeterminate. Because ranks are discrete and computed against the complete 64 pairs, report point rank plus bootstrap distribution, never a fabricated sampling-population p-value.
- Held-out 200 instructions are **fixed**, not conditionally increased after looking at precision. Report prompt exclusions, baselines, raw margins, continuous route fractions, all pair results, and per-seed cases even when gates fail.
- All intervention settings, patching directions, hash identities, thresholds, condition order, scoring definitions, and software revisions frozen **before** primary confirmatory measurements. Any violations recorded; any unregistered analysis labelled exploratory.
- No model rerun after outcomes solely to improve a hypothesis class. Scientific early stop only by predeclared rule on exploratory models; no confirmatory outcome-based early stop.

## 12. Clean adapter comparison — RESOLVED: DEFERRED

`interpretable-finetuning/gemma-clean` publishes 15 no-poison adapters, but Agent 1's read-only inspection of the model card, `clean_index.json`, and checkpoint configuration establishes that they are **top-k sparse** (`use_topk: true`, `k=8`, `relu_latents: true`, `reg_mode: z_only`). Correct loading requires a custom `TopKLoRALinearSTE` wrapper and reloading method reported to be in a **currently private** repository; stock PEFT may load the weights but evaluate the wrong model. The clean and dense backdoored checkpoints are also not step-matched (3,750 versus 3,939 reported steps).

The clean adapters are therefore **not a matched negative control for Q1** and are **deferred** (§1). A clean adapter's backdoor trigger effect `T` may be near zero, making route-effect ratios unstable or undefined. The adapter-disabled base reference already supplies an exactly shared upstream, non-backdoored baseline for `l19`, though it does **not** provide an otherwise matched clean fine-tuning control. If future appropriately matched **dense clean** checkpoints become available, a prospectively designed comparison of unnormalized tag-readout effects would be a different/additional study, not an automatic extension of Q1.
